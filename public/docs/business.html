Business thesis

EOB Capital positions itself as a regulated deep-tech development hub (KYC-DYN8). LETZZ Wallet and LETZZ Platform are the reference product family that demonstrates how individuals and obliged entities can exchange KYC and AML-relevant evidence with consent, auditability, and respect for the person’s vault.

The wallet is a reference sample and commercial path, not the entire definition of the company. Strategy materials therefore speak carefully: they describe product capability and commercial intent without claiming finished supervisory certifications before those paths are complete.

Document discipline. Figures from partner meetings are labelled as meeting narrative. Euro list prices are intentionally unset until unit cost per user (denoted X) is known. This brief does not invent prices.

Two customers of one capability

Commercial design treats the natural person as the primary beneficiary of control, and the obliged entity as the secondary buyer of reliable, permissioned access to supporting evidence.

Primary - individual (and corporate-linked individuals) Stores life documents; answers requests; may share proactively; may invite guests. Corporate sponsors can pay for linked individual wallets (board, inceptors) without absorbing those wallets into a company vault.
Secondary - obliged entity Pays for the right to operate cases and, under terms, to view or download supporting documents required for AML and KYC duties and retention - after the person grants the relevant scope.

Revenue design

Philosophy from the 27 October 2026 commercial discussion: prefer fixed and predictable fees; make the first sale as frictionless as possible; introduce upsells after trust is established.

Wallet line

  • Individual wallets - paid by the person or by a sponsoring corporate.
  • Corporate wallets - company pays for the corporate wallet and for linked individual wallets.
  • Inceptor wallets - additional revenue for compliance staff who initiate and steward corporate onboarding.

Obliged-entity line

  1. Permission first. The wallet owner grants the organisation permission to view selected wallet data (not underlying document bytes by default).
  2. Base service. After general terms, the organisation pays for rights to view and download supporting documents needed for AML/KYC obligations and retention.
  3. Optional premium. Paid reliance on EOB’s KYC assessment at substantial or high level - only when that assessment product is licensed and contracted. Not claimed as shipped in this demo hub.
Unit economics. Euro prices remain unset. The model is built around X = cost per user until measured unit economics support a published tariff. Do not treat any figure in this brief as a quotation.

Go-to-market posture

  • Seed commercial motion: fixed-fee conversations with early obliged entities and beta wallets; emphasise predictability and evidence integrity.
  • Pilot shape: scoped tenant, synthetic or consented data, named support runbook, and explicit honesty about which providers are live.
  • Cash-flow aspiration (meeting narrative): day-one revenue after ILNAS notification and EU Trusted List entry, assuming Big Four pilots and beta wallets - contingent on that path completing; not asserted as already done.
  • Partner hub: this Alma-gated static site is for controlled previews. It is not a public marketing site and not production onboarding.

Risks and integrity commitments

  • Do not market unfinished screening, mesh, or cloud key recovery as live.
  • Do not present this GitHub Pages hub as production authentication or hosting.
  • Do not claim AML6, eIDAS/EUDI, or ILNAS/QTSP certification before Trusted List entry and owner clearance.
  • Do not invent euro prices from X.
  • Keep language respectful and suitable for institutional and faith-sensitive audiences.
  • Preserve distinct product states so commercial conversations never blur consent with clearance.
